Film preview

The film is built from the timeline file: the clips in recording order, the clap and the camera movement cut out, a hard cut between scenes, the sound crossfaded, the film faded in and out. The preview plays the small preview copies, not the 4K film.

no preview

Look and sound

The base look (Blackmagic Gen 5 Film to Video) is always applied. A creative LUT goes on top of it, for the whole film. The sound of every scene is levelled to the same loudness, then the whole film to the target, with a limit for the true peak.

Export

The final film is rendered from the original BRAW files, not from the preview copies, so it takes a while (about half real time on this machine). The job runs here; its progress is shown above.

Scenes

Move the start or the end of a scene by one second or one frame, remove a scene (untick "In film"), or change the order. A removed scene stays in the list, greyed, with a Restore button. Every change is saved to the timeline file at once and the old file is kept. Click a row to jump to it in the preview. After a change, render the preview again.

Subtitles

Write the words of a scene in its box (type them, or dictate them with Superwhisper). Press Save text: the tool removes filler words such as um and uh, fixes spacing and capitals, splits the text into subtitles that fit two lines, and spreads them across the scene in proportion to their length. Then drag a subtitle to move it, drag its edges to change its length, or type its times. Saving the box again splits only that scene, and asks first if you moved subtitles by hand.

Translations

Translate the English subtitles into other languages and write one SRT file per language next to the film (film.pt-BR.srt and so on), for YouTube. Every translation has the times of its English subtitle. The text is sent to OpenRouter; the key is read from the environment variable OPENROUTER_API_KEY, or from the macOS keychain, and is never shown or written.

Titles

A title is text drawn into the picture of every export, in Inter, with a fade. Times are film seconds.

Sync offset per clip

The offset is how many seconds later a sound comes in the recorder's file than in the camera's. The tool measured one for every clip when it matched the files. If one is wrong, type the right one in seconds (a minus sign is allowed). Only the sound of that clip's scenes changes; the pictures stay as they are. Empty the field, or press Original, to use the measured offset again. The measured offset is never overwritten.
